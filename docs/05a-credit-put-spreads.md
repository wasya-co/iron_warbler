

I think it's reasonable to size the spread as a percentage of the price. I am aggressive in my plays, and I've noticed that my spreads are 10% of the price. So if a stock XYZ is trading at $100, I'd enter a $120-130 credit call spread. Or is a stock ABC is trading at $600, I'd enter $720-780 credit call spread.

I'm roughly, roughly assuming that a percent is a delta point. I know it's wrong - but first, I don't have the machinery to compute my own delta right now, and second, if my upstream platform messes up or becomes unavailable, or if I need to do calculation in my head, percent actually go a very long way. Just use percent for everything instead of delta.

Same can be roughly said about dollar amounts rather than delta. I trade rather consistently, and let's say I expect to enter a spread for a $2.00-2.50 profit, and close it for $0.97. In other words, I am looking for a reasonable entrance, and an exit for under one dollar. But this also means that if I'm placing an order and it says $6 credit, I know something is wrong. I know the volatility has gone through the rough, and I need to move faaaar away from the current price, reduce the delta. Again: a very mechanical process, I don't even need to compute the deltas, if I am used to, and monitor, the common prices.
