
= credit vs debit spreads =

In my strategies, I only deal with credit spreads. I tried debit spreads, and somehow, only lost money and I'm just not going to do them anymore. I see little value proposition in debit spreads, like I see little proposition in buying options. On-net most of my open transactions are sales. The transaction to close, in this case of course, is a purchase.

I have available capital and I'm using it to open credit spreads. Let's just focus on this, without distracting outselves with all the other possibilities. From this point of view, the bearish position must be a call spread. The bullish position is a put spread.

\<>

~ * ~ * ~ * ~

With that said, there are reasons to think of debit spreads. Let's say my position is under-water and I want to roll it slightly. I want to reduce the risk of the inner leg of a credit call spread (bearish). Such a transaction by itself is a debit spread.

\<>

So let's say TSLA is trading at $390 and I have a $390-430 credit call spread, I am uncomfortable and I want to move to a $395-430 call spread. This is the transaction to do so, essentially a debit spread.

\<> 20261112 [ref-0]

And I naturally have to ask myself: is it worth it? Do I *believe* in such a transaction? It says, I'd pay $2.50, and it has to be above $392.50 to break even. My max gain is a hefty 50%. I don't believe in this transaction, but the potential payout is big, and potential loss (100%) is big, and the loss is there even if I don't place this trade. Since this would be a change to an existing position, it's not just whether or not I'd place this trade by itself, right?

Naturally I'd ask myself: what's the opposite of this transaction. Because if I'm not placing this trade, for risk purposes it is as if I'm placing the opposite trade, no? It is as if I hold a $395-430 call spread, and I'm rolling it to $390-430:

\<> 20261112 [ref-1]

Here, I'd be selling a bearish spread for $2.50, breaking even at less than $392.50. Do I believe in such a transaction? Actually no, not today, either. The question then becomes: if I believe in neither transaction, which one do I chose? I should chose to minimize my risk, rather than maximize the reward.

What I'm going to do is, since I have liquidity to enter 2 of these, I'm doing to enter only 1. This way I can think about it some more, and collect the data. I'm doing both: entering and not entering, because I'm utilizing half of the liquidity, and making half of th decision. Or not... in either case, the total risk for this or whatever, is $250 per unit. I have liquidity for 2, so max I'm gaining is $500, max I'm losing is $500 as well. And actually, since I have other TSLA positions which are long, I am net long, then I should increase the short position. The reader is invited to go through similar reasoning steps with their own trades.
