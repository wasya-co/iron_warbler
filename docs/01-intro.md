There is a book, Options as a Strategic Investment. It is a big and boring book, which is good. It is detailed, with lots of food for thought. It is slightly obsolete - some mechanics haven't changed, but some did, so you have to read it at your own risk. On the other hand, it is timestamped in the past, so to speak, and the modern craziness didn't get into this old book. So you can get a glimpse of a different world, of a much less volatile market, but reading it. Overall, I found the book very useful. I don't agree with everything that it says. I'm mentioning it here because you may find if useful, too.

There is a book called, Reminiscences of a Stock Operator. A very good book, and I recommend it. I won't go into details here, it's an autobiography of Lewis, one of the best traders of all time. It was written, and he lived, in a time when the markets were much, much less regulated. By "operator" he means "manipulator." By the way, he killed himself - this best trader of all time. Not only because he was brilliant, but also because mental problems run in his family. Anyway, a very good book, and relevant to our discussion here.
