    * the necessity of credit call spreads in a "balanced" portfolio
      By "balanced" I actually mean, completely psychotic. But the psychotic portfolio needs to make money in all events, in all market conditions.
      So what's the catch, you ask? You had a portfolio that used to make money only if markets go up or are flat. You now have a portfolio that (allegedly) makes money if markets are up, flat, or down. What is the cost of that?
      The answer: the cost is the opportunity lost. As a reminder, when I trade and "lose", I am thinking of it two-ways: Am I losing money? Or am I losing opportunity? So, in the defended portfolio, I continuously lose opportunity, by mis-allocating funds to a branch that I know will be losing (non-making) money. The cost of this strategy is the opportunity cost of this branch of the position.
